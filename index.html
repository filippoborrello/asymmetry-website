What is Asymmetry?

Getting involved in economics research as an undergraduate is hard. The barrier to entry is high, and building relationships with faculty takes time. At the same time, the kind of writing you find in The Economist or the Financial Times, which applies economics to real-world topics, is largely missing on campus.

Asymmetry is a place for that writing. Inspired by Equilibrium at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, we publish an annual review of student work, plus shorter pieces throughout the year. Every piece is peer reviewed, and long-form pieces aim for faculty review too.

No experience is needed. Interdisciplinary topics are welcome; it doesn't have to be strictly economics or politics.

What you can write

Analysis

An original research piece in the style of an academic journal article. More data oriented, with visualizations and, ideally, help from faculty.

Opinion

Any topic that interests you, with more room for creative structure. Think trade wars, the housing market, or a policy debate.

Research review

Pick one published paper and walk readers through it: first what it says, then your own reflection and its policy implications.

How long?

Long form

1,000–1,500 words

  • 2,000 words maximum, about a 7 minute read
  • Two peer reviews and one faculty review
  • Published in the spring edition

Short form

Up to 500 words

  • Often tied to current events, but doesn't have to be
  • One peer review
  • Published on completion
  • Open to writers working on a long-form piece

From idea to publication

  1. 01

    Proposal

    Two or three topics, each with a research question, a short paragraph on why, how economics applies, and five sources. Due October 31.

  2. 02

    Literature review

    Read what's been written, both academic and journalistic.

  3. 03

    Peer review 1

    Finalize the proposal and outline the concepts. Mid November.

  4. 04

    Drafting

    Outline and preliminary draft, then a second peer review in early January.

  5. 05

    Final draft and faculty review

    Final draft by late January, faculty review in February.

  6. 06

    Publishing

    The review is published in the spring.
