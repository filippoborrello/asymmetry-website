What is Asymmetry?

Getting involved in economics research as an undergraduate is hard. The barrier to entry is high, and building relationships with faculty takes time. At the same time, the kind of writing you find in The Economist or the Financial Times, which applies economics to real-world topics, is largely missing on campus.

Asymmetry is a place for that writing. Inspired by Equilibrium at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, we publish an annual spring review of student work, plus short takes throughout the year. Every piece is peer reviewed, and long-form pieces aim for faculty review too.

Write for Asymmetry

Analysis

An original, data-driven piece in the style of an academic journal article, with visualizations and, ideally, faculty guidance.

Opinion

Any topic you care about, with room for creative structure. Trade wars, housing, a policy debate.

Research Review

Walk readers through one published paper, then give your own reflection and its policy implications.

Length

Long form runs 1,000–1,500 words (2,000 max) in the spring issue. Short takes are up to 500 words and run when ready.

Have an idea? Write it.

No experience needed. Send two or three topics, each with a research question and a few sources, and we’ll pair you with peer editors.
